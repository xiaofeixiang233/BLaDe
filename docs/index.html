Bayesian Language Decision

Looking Back and Planning Ahead

A fast actor chooses every action; a slow planner speaks only now and then. BLaDe asks the same frozen actor three questions at each step and combines the answers with Bayes' rule, so recent experience sets the prior and the planner's desired future pulls on it with one weight, w.

Method

A prior from the past.
Evidence from the planned future.

Scoring how likely each action is to reach the planner's target would need a world model. Under a simple factorization, Bayes' rule turns that likelihood into a ratio of action probabilities the actor already returns.

Look back

History sets the prior

The actor reads the observation plus the last four real action–outcome pairs. This is what it would do given what just happened.

πh(a) = π(a | ot, ht)
Plan ahead

The target adds evidence

A System II planner proposes a desired future state at most four times per episode. Dividing by the observation-only view keeps just the shift the target causes.

πp(a) / π0(a)
Compose

One weight, then act

The ratio is raised to w and multiplied into the prior. w = 0 ignores the plan; larger w lets the plan override the past. The top action is executed once.

at = argmax π̃w(a)

π̃w(a) ∝ π(a | ot, ht) · [π(a | ot, pt)π(a | ot)]w
